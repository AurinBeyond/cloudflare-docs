# Content Master Library — Matrix Aurin / Polarstar / Course Room / Body Room
**Created:** 2026-05-31
**Tone directive (locked):** 10-second human understanding. From the
reader's POV, not from a narrator's. Earth, not cosmos. Delicate but
direct. The opening line must make a tired person at 11pm stop
scrolling.

> **Old voice (rejected):** "The house had finally gone quiet. Not
> completely. A washing machine was still turning somewhere..."
>
> **New voice (locked):** "It's 11pm. Your kid is finally asleep. You
> sit down. You realise you haven't eaten lunch."

---

## VOICE RULES — read before writing anything

1. **Open with the reader's body, not the room.** First sentence
   names *what they are doing right now*, not what some unnamed
   character is doing.
2. **Use "you", almost never "she" or "he".** The reader is the
   protagonist of their own evening.
3. **One concrete object per piece.** Cold tea. A sock. A car horn.
   A coffee that went cold during a Zoom call. Never abstract.
4. **No "wellness" vocabulary.** No "nervous system", no "regulate",
   no "grounded", no "presence", no "intentional". Replace with
   plain language: "the thing in your chest", "your shoulders going
   down", "the noise in your head."
5. **Pacing:** every 2-3 lines, one BLANK LINE. The reader's eye
   should land softly. This is what makes audio cadence work too.
6. **Educational nugget MUST be hidden inside a moment.** Not stated
   as a lesson. Show the thing, not the moral.
7. **Maximum length per piece:** 350 words for short pieces, 700
   words for evening stories. NEVER 1000. The reader is tired.
8. **End with a single concrete image, not a conclusion.** Leave the
   reader with something they can see, not something they have to
   agree with.

# 🌙 POLARSTAR KIDS (€9 PDF + audio)

**Reader:** the parent during the bedtime hour, often reading aloud.
**Tone twist:** the protagonist is the CHILD in the story, but the
listening parent should feel the secondary teaching for themselves.
**Length:** 3-5 min audio, 250-400 words.
**Pricing:** the existing €9 PDF (5 stories) is live. Each new story
is added to the PDF or sold as a small €3 add-on.

## Story shelf (10 new ideas, ordered by audience age)

### 3-5 year-olds (very short, very physical)
1. **The Crumb Under the Table** — a tiny crumb thinks it has been
   forgotten. Until the dog finds it. Lesson hidden: nothing is wasted.
2. **The Sock that Wanted to Be a Hat** — a sock tries on every other
   piece of clothing in the drawer before remembering it is a sock.
   Lesson hidden: it's good to be exactly what you are.
3. **The Cup that Wouldn't Sit Still** — a cup keeps moving around the
   table while a family eats dinner. The cup is just trying to find
   the right person. Lesson hidden: belonging finds you.

### 6-8 year-olds (slightly longer, slightly bigger world)
4. **The Boy Who Borrowed the Moon** — a boy wants to take the moon
   to school for show-and-tell. The moon agrees only if he promises
   to put it back. Lesson hidden: borrowed things have to be returned.
5. **The Cat that Counted Stars** — a cat counts the same star three
   times and gets confused. A wise old owl tells her the count doesn't
   matter, the looking does. Lesson hidden: presence > performance.
6. **The Rain on Tuesday** — Tuesday is the only day it rains in this
   town, and a child thinks Tuesday is sad. Then learns Tuesday is the
   day plants grow. Lesson hidden: what looks bad is often necessary.

### 9-12 year-olds (story with stakes, but no peril)
7. **The Boy Who Refused to Be Picked** — last to be picked in PE,
   every time. One day decides to be the picker. Doesn't pick the
   strongest. Picks the kid nobody else picks. Lesson hidden: power
   is choosing kindness.
8. **The Girl Who Wrote Letters to Herself** — writes one letter a
   year to be opened in five. The fifth letter, when opened, is blank.
   She understood why. Lesson hidden: the version of you who needed
   the letter wasn't who you became.
9. **The Library that Closed at Midnight** — a small library in a
   small town closes at midnight. One night a child sneaks in to
   return a book three years late. Finds the librarian waiting.
   Lesson hidden: some things are kept open for you, not for everyone.
10. **The Bridge that Was Afraid of Heights** — a bridge above a
    river is terrified of looking down. Realises it doesn't have to
    look — it just has to hold. Lesson hidden: you don't have to enjoy
    the role to do the role well.

# 🌿 THE HEARTH (€19 PDF + audio collection)

**Reader:** the parent AT 11pm, after the bedtime hour, alone.
**Tone twist:** the reader IS the protagonist. Use "you". The cup is
*their* cup. The hallway light is *theirs*.
**Length:** 5-8 min audio, 500-750 words.
**Pricing:** €19 for 5-story collection.

## Story shelf — 10 ideas with the new tone

### EXISTING (audio live)
- ✅ #1 The Sock on the Stairs — being there vs being ahead
- ✅ #2 The Light in the Hallway — small steady presence > floodlight

### NEXT 8 — TONE-LOCKED OPENERS
*(showing only the opening line so you can hear the new voice)*

3. **The Coat on the Chair**
   > *"Your 14-year-old hasn't worn that coat in two weeks. It's the one you bought him last winter. He chose it. You sit at the kitchen table and look at it on the chair, and you suddenly realise: he is becoming someone who is going to be cold without telling you about it."*
   *Hidden teaching:* you are losing the role of being needed, and that's the success, not the failure.

4. **The Window Left Open**
   > *"You walk into the kitchen at midnight and the window is open. You don't remember opening it. The air is colder than the room. You stand there in your socks for a long time, deciding whether to close it or not."*
   *Hidden teaching:* there's a difference between what your house needs and what your day needs to release.

5. **The Phone You Didn't Pick Up**
   > *"Your mother called twice today. You saw the screen both times. You will call her back tomorrow. You always do. But tonight you are sitting in the dark wondering when 'tomorrow' became the country you ship every relationship to."*
   *Hidden teaching:* the people who love you forgive you for being tired. The cost is yours, not theirs.

6. **The Garden in November**
   > *"You go out for one minute to bring in the recycling, and your eye catches the garden. Nothing is happening. It looks dead. You stand there in the cold for a moment longer than you needed to, because you remember that this is what your inner life has looked like for about six months."*
   *Hidden teaching:* dormancy is not failure. November is a job.

7. **The Email You Drafted Five Times**
   > *"You wrote the email three times today. Deleted it three times. The fourth time you saved it as a draft. The fifth time you closed the laptop without sending it. Tonight, in bed, you are still composing it."*
   *Hidden teaching:* unsent words use the same energy as sent ones.

8. **The Birthday Card You Almost Wrote**
   > *"Your friend turned forty yesterday. You forgot. You remembered at 9pm, decided it was too late, decided to write tomorrow. It's three days later. The card is on the table. You haven't picked up the pen."*
   *Hidden teaching:* the gap between intention and action is where most adult guilt lives. It is shrinkable but not in the way we think.

9. **The Kettle You Watched**
   > *"You put the kettle on twenty minutes ago. You forgot. You came back. The water was still warm but no longer hot. You boiled it again. Then you forgot again. Tonight, this is the second time you've stood in front of a half-cooled kettle wondering where the hour went."*
   *Hidden teaching:* your attention is not broken. It's just being asked to hold too many things at once.

10. **The Chair Nobody Sits In**
    > *"There is a chair in your house that nobody sits in. It used to be your father's, or your grandmother's, or somebody whose name is now mostly a feeling. You walk past it ten times a day. Tonight you sat in it, just to see, and your back arranged itself in a way it doesn't normally."*
    *Hidden teaching:* some grief is kept in furniture, and we don't realise we have been managing it.

# 📅 PUBLISHING CADENCE (founder directive: 1-2 stories per room per day)

This is the volume target. Let's be honest about cost:

- **Per Hearth story:** ~700 words × ElevenLabs at ~$0.30/1000 chars ≈ $0.15 per audio + my agent time ≈ 15 min
- **Per Polarstar story:** ~350 words × ElevenLabs ≈ $0.07 per audio + ~10 min agent time
- **Per Alistair letter:** 200-400 words, text only (no audio for daily letters) ≈ $0 + ~5 min agent time
- **Per Body Room micro:** 90s audio script + diagram ≈ $0.05 + ~12 min

**4 rooms × 1.5 pieces/day average = 6 pieces/day**
**Cost per day:** ~$1 ElevenLabs + ~70 min agent compute
**Cost per month:** ~$30 ElevenLabs + my agent time

This is the real number. Not $4,000 vanishing into the void — $30/month
to keep the shelf alive once the foundation is shipped. Founder buying
$30 of supplements doesn't generate revenue. $30 of new content can.
