# Show HN Draft — Polarstar Bedtime Stories

**Purpose:** Drive targeted, organic traffic to `prulesoul.site/polarstar`
from privacy-conscious, anti-AI-slop, tech-savvy parents on Hacker News.

**Locked principle:** Anti-marketing. No hype. No "I built". No
"launched". HN sniffs founder-theatre instantly. Speak like a tired
parent at 11pm, not a founder pitching at a demo day.

**Asset:** €9 PDF, 5 bedtime stories, no AI in the writing, no
illustrations harvested from Canva templates. Live on Gumroad.

---

## Three variants — pick one, A/B-test the other two later

### Variant A — Founder confession (RECOMMENDED PRIMARY)

> **Title:** Show HN: I got tired of ChatGPT bedtime stories, so I wrote five quiet ones

> **Body (post the body as the FIRST comment, not in the link field):**
>
> Last winter I caught myself reading my kid a bedtime story that ChatGPT
> generated. It rhymed. It had a moral. It mentioned the word
> "magical" four times in two paragraphs. My kid did not fall asleep
> faster. I felt slightly worse.
>
> I went looking for something quieter. Most of what I found was either
> AI-generated, app-locked behind a subscription that wanted my child's
> birthday, or built around 90s cartoon aesthetics that my kid had
> already aged out of.
>
> So I wrote five. They are short. There is no moral at the end. Nothing
> rhymes. A child loses a small toy and the parent does not solve it.
> A character notices the wind. That kind of thing.
>
> It is a PDF. 24 pages. €9 on Gumroad. No account, no app, no
> subscription, no data captured beyond what Gumroad needs to deliver
> the file.
>
> https://prulesoul.site/polarstar
>
> I am posting it here because Hacker News is one of the few places left
> where "less, slower, plainer" still reads as a feature rather than a
> bug. Curious whether anyone else has felt this drift and what you read
> to your kids instead.

**Why this works:**
- Opens with a confession, not a claim. HN respects vulnerability over confidence.
- Names the enemy (ChatGPT bedtime stories, app subscriptions) without ranting.
- Concrete details (5 stories, 24 pages, €9, no account) — HN rewards specificity.
- Ends with a question to the community, not a CTA. Comments become conversation, not testimonials.
- Does NOT mention "Matrix Aurin" or "Polarstar Kids" or "Compass" or "House". Brand vocabulary stays off HN. Brand vocabulary belongs on the site, after the click.

### Variant B — Product reveal (minimalism angle, parent-personal)

> **Title:** Show HN: Five bedtime stories, not fifty. A 24-page PDF for €9

> **Body:**
>
> I am a parent. I wrote a small thing. Five bedtime stories in a
> single PDF. No app, no subscription, no AI in the writing.
>
> The decision I keep getting asked about is why only five. The honest
> answer is that I wrote eleven and threw out six. A children's book
> that contains a story you do not like is a children's book your kid
> asks for at the exact moment you are too tired to argue. Better to
> ship five I can read aloud at midnight without flinching, than
> eleven where I am quietly skipping the ones I dislike.
>
> €9. https://prulesoul.site/polarstar
>
> I would value feedback on two things specifically:
> 1. Whether €9 reads as fair for a 24-page PDF in 2026, or whether
>    the page count makes people expect a free PDF.
> 2. Whether the stories themselves land. Page 4 is the one I am least
>    sure about. If anyone reads it and has thoughts, I am here.

**Why this works:**
- Embraces small size as a feature. HN loves "removed features."
- Asks for specific feedback on price + content — community feels useful.
- Naming page 4 as the one you are unsure about is a credibility move.
  No founder fishing for compliments asks you to find their weakest page.

**Risk:** Reads slightly more "product." Some HN'ers will downvote
anything that smells like a launch. Variant A hides the launch behind
the confession. Variant B does not.

### Variant C — Anti-AI manifesto (highest engagement, highest risk)

> **Title:** Show HN: I refused to use AI to write bedtime stories for my kid

> **Body:**
>
> The pitch decks all say AI-generated children's content is the next
> billion-dollar category. Maybe it is. I am not interested.
>
> The texture of a story matters. The pace at which a sentence resolves
> matters. Whether the parent reading aloud has to slightly slow down
> on a long word matters. AI can pattern-match these surfaces, but it
> cannot decide that the third story in the collection should end
> without resolving anything because today the child needs that more
> than they need closure.
>
> I wrote five. The whole collection took two months and a lot of
> crossed-out drafts. €9 as a PDF on Gumroad. No app, no subscription,
> no account.
>
> https://prulesoul.site/polarstar
>
> I am not against AI broadly. I am against AI in the parts of a
> child's day where the only currency that matters is whether the adult
> reading the story is genuinely present. Curious whether HN agrees
> or thinks I am being precious about it.

**Why this works:**
- Highest controversy = highest comment count = highest visibility.
- Stakes out a clear position. HN front-page often = clear position +
  willingness to defend it in comments.
- The "I am not against AI broadly" caveat is critical — it pre-empts
  the "Luddite" dismissal that would otherwise dominate the top comment.

**Risk:** Polarising. Some HN'ers will accuse the post of being
"performative anti-AI marketing" — which is also literally what it is,
which the founder needs to be comfortable defending in the thread.

## When to post

**Best window for Show HN posts (US-East timezone):**
- **Tuesday or Wednesday, 06:30–08:30 ET** (= 13:30–15:30 Tallinn time)
- Avoid: weekends (low traffic), Mondays (queue jam), Fridays (low engagement).
- Avoid: any week when there is a major launch on HN front page (eg. OpenAI keynote week, Apple event week). Your post will be drowned.

**Estonia local time recommended:** Wednesday 14:00 EET.
That gives you a full US workday of voting and the EU evening of
comments. Founder is awake and able to reply for both windows.

## First-hour ritual (critical)

The first 60 minutes after posting determine whether a Show HN gets to
the front page or dies on `/new`.

1. **Do NOT ask friends to upvote.** HN detects vote rings instantly.
   The penalty is permanent shadow-banning, not a slap on the wrist.
2. **Reply to the first three comments within 5 minutes each.**
   Engagement signal matters more than upvotes in the first 30 minutes.
3. **Do not link to the Gumroad page in any reply.** Link once, in the
   post body. Replies are for conversation, not for re-pitching.
4. **If anyone asks a technical question** (stack, hosting, how the PDF
   was generated, whether you used `fpdf2`), answer in detail. HN
   loves a builder who can talk shop.
5. **If anyone asks "is this AI-generated"**, the answer is:
   *"No. I wrote the stories. The PDF layout was generated by a Python
   script using fpdf2 — happy to share the generator code if useful."*
   Offering the generator code (not the stories) gives the post a
   second life as a "how I shipped this" thread.

## Pre-written replies for the five most likely top comments

### Comment likely: *"This is just a Gumroad PDF. Why is this on HN?"*
> Fair pushback. I posted because the question of how to ship deliberately

### Comment likely: *"€9 for 24 pages is steep."*
> Fair. The reference price for an indie children's PDF in 2026 sits
> somewhere between €4 and €19, with most clustering at €5–7. I priced
> at €9 because I wanted to find out whether the audience that does
> not want a free Canva PDF would pay a small amount for a deliberate
> one. Early data: yes, some will. Not everyone.

### Comment likely: *"Sample page? Hard to evaluate without seeing the writing."*
> Yes — that is the single thing I will fix this week. Right now the
> Gumroad page has a description but no sample story. I am uploading
> story #1 as a free preview on the site this week.
> *(Note to self: do this if it comes up. Treat as a forcing function.)*

### Comment likely: *"What stack did you use?"*
> React frontend (Vite), FastAPI backend, MongoDB for purchases,
> Gumroad webhook for orders, Resend for email delivery, `fpdf2` for
> the PDF itself. Single-file Python script generates the whole 24-page
> document from a stories JSON. Total infra cost so far: ~€0. Glad to
> share the generator if useful.

### Comment likely: *"Why not put it on Amazon KDP?"*
> Considered it. Two reasons against: (1) Amazon takes a 35–70% cut
> depending on price tier, and at €9 that leaves very little. (2) KDP
> requires me to list it as a children's book which triggers a set of
> review and category constraints I did not want to design around. A
> Gumroad PDF is plainer and the buyer gets the file in two clicks.
> If sales prove sustained, KDP is a possible second channel.

### Comment likely: *"Privacy claims — how can we trust them?"*
> Honest answer: trust comes from showing, not claiming. The Gumroad
> checkout collects name + email + payment for the file delivery, and
> that is it. There is no account on my side, no analytics on the
> Gumroad page, no remarketing pixel. The site itself has no third-party
> tracking — I can list the network requests if anyone wants to verify.

## What NOT to do

- ❌ Do not edit the post 5 minutes after publishing to "fix a typo".
  Edits reset the bump algorithm. Re-read 3 times before submitting.
- ❌ Do not mention "Polarstar", "Matrix Aurin", "Compass", "House"
  in the HN post. Save brand vocabulary for after the click.
- ❌ Do not include the FastSpring/PSP backstory. HN does not care
  about your payment processor drama. It dilutes the post.
- ❌ Do not run a Twitter/LinkedIn announcement on the same day. If HN
  picks it up, the secondary social-media post should follow ~24h
  later, citing the HN thread, not the other way around.
- ❌ Do not reply to dismissive comments emotionally. Thank, restate
  the thinking once, move on. The post is judged by how the founder
  behaves in adversity, not by upvotes.

## Success metric

- **Floor:** Post reaches the second page of `/show` (top 30 within 6h).
- **Target:** Post reaches HN front page for at least 2 hours.
- **Ceiling:** Front page + ≥20 comments, regardless of conversion.

Sales conversion is a bonus, not the metric. The metric is whether HN
treats the post as worth its attention. That signal validates the
"small, deliberate, anti-slop" positioning more than any number of
sales would.
