## Page 2 — A letter to the parent (~250 words)

The house has just gone quiet.

The last footstep on the stairs has stilled. The light under the
children's door is off. You are sitting in the room where the day
ends — kitchen, hallway, edge of the bed — and your body is finally
not needed for the first time since six o'clock this morning.

And yet, somehow, you are not free.

There is a particular kind of exhaustion that arrives at this hour.
It is not the exhaustion of doing too much. It is the exhaustion of
having been on call for fourteen hours, of having decoded forty small
emotional weather systems that are not yours, of having held the
operating system of a home steady while pretending that holding it is
not a job.

The next twenty minutes are not free time. They are the only window in
which you are not on call. Most evenings, those twenty minutes get lost
to scrolling, or to nothing at all. This is a protocol for those twenty
minutes.

It is not therapy. It is not parenting advice. It is not a course you
take or a streak you maintain. It is one short sequence you can run,
end to end, every evening that needs it. You own it. There is no
version 2.

If even one of the next twenty minutes belongs more clearly to you
tonight than it did yesterday, the protocol has done its work.

— Anna

## Page 3 — How to use this protocol (~120 words)

Read this once, slowly, by lamplight. Then either:

- Print it. Keep the printed page by the bed. Run the protocol from
  the paper.
- Or, if reading at this hour is already too much, press play on
  *Hearth Audio Companion* (track 1). The audio is the same words,
  read aloud, at the same pace.

The protocol is twenty minutes. Six small stages, none of them long.
Skip any stage that does not fit tonight. The protocol does not
break.

Once finished, the *Inheritance Inventory* worksheet (a separate page
in this bundle) gives you space to write down what came up — but only
if writing helps you tonight. Some nights it will. Some nights it
won't.

The ambient soundscape (*One Quiet Evening*, track 2) is optional. It
loops. Use it underneath the protocol, or after.

## Page 4 — Minutes 1–3: The Closure Signal

Light one small light.

Not the overhead lamp. Not the phone. A candle, a salt lamp, a single
soft bulb — something whose only job is to mark this twenty minutes.

This light is the closure signal. Your nervous system has spent the
day reading micro-cues: a child's intake of breath, the small change
in tone of a partner, the click of the front door. It is not stupid;
it is the cleverest part of you. It will read this signal too, if you
give it consistently.

Sit somewhere you can see the light. Do not yet do anything else.

For ninety seconds: simply look at the light. Notice that it is small.
Notice that it does not require anything from you.

You are no longer on call.

The protocol begins now.

---

## Page 5 — Minutes 4–8: The Inheritance Inventory

Below are six questions. Read each one. Allow a small pause. Do not
answer aloud, do not write yet — only let the question be heard
inside you.

If a question lands with weight, stay there longer. If a question
does not land tonight, move on. There is no order to defend.

1. *What did my parents hand me today, without saying so?*
2. *What did I hand my children today, without meaning to?*
3. *What did I outsource today that I would rather have done myself?*
4. *What did I do today that I would rather have outsourced?*
5. *What is still open between me and one specific person?*
6. *What is one small thing the household no longer needs me to carry?*

None of these are graded.
None of these are shared.
None of these are about being a better parent tomorrow.

They are the inventory of one specific evening. Run them again the
next evening if the next evening asks for it.

---

## Page 6 — Minutes 9–12: One Loop on Paper

Pick one of the six questions whose answer is still ringing.

Write that answer on a single line on a single piece of paper. Any
paper. Any pen.

One line. No expansion. No explanation.

Then place the paper somewhere out of sight — a drawer, a book, a
folded jacket pocket. Not the bin. Not the desk where work happens.
Somewhere quiet.

The mind cannot close a loop it is still rehearsing. Writing one
sentence on paper is the smallest version of closing it. The paper
holds the loop now. You do not have to.

You will not need to look at the paper again. If you do, you may.
That is also fine.

---

## Page 7 — Minutes 13–16: One Body Sweep

Find one place in your body that is still holding the day's first
small frustration.

Not the biggest one. The first one. The one you barely noticed at
half past seven this morning when you were already running.

It is probably in your shoulders, your jaw, the back of your neck, or
the soft place behind your sternum. Different days, different places.

Place your hand on that place, or rest your attention there if your
hand does not want to move.

Soften the place by a small amount. Not all at once. A small amount.

Notice the small amount.

Stay there for as long as the small amount stays interesting. Then
move on.

---

## Page 8 — Minutes 17–19: One Quiet Thing

For three minutes, do one quiet thing that the Anchor OS has not
chosen for itself today.

It is not the same thing every evening.

Tonight it might be: the first three pages of a book that has been
sitting on the shelf for a month. Or opening a window for a minute,
even if it is cold. Or pouring a single cup of something whose taste
you actually like, instead of finishing the cold cup left from
dinner. Or simply sitting and not doing anything, looking at the
small light.

Three minutes. Choose for yourself. The household does not need to
know.

---

## Page 9 — Minute 20: The hearth is yours

Blow out the light. Or turn it off, if it is a lamp.

That movement marks the end of the twenty minutes.

The hearth is yours. The protocol is complete. Whatever happens next
— sleep, more wakefulness, reading, lying still with the eyes open —
is allowed.

(Small visual: the same candle, now extinguished, a thin trace of
smoke.)

---

## Page 10 — When the protocol does not work

Some nights, the protocol will not close the loops. The loops will
win. The mind will keep running. Sleep will not come.

The protocol does not promise sleep.

It promises twenty minutes that are yours — even if sleep does not
arrive after them.

That is enough.

On the nights when the protocol does not work, do not punish
yourself. Do not "try harder." Do not double the inventory or extend
the body sweep. The protocol is the same length and the same shape
every night. Its consistency is its kindness.

Tomorrow you will run it again. Or you will not. The protocol does
not require anything of you that it has not already given.

— Anna

   the agent's voice is gentler-American-confessional, yours is

   yours, not the agent's.

   adult house palette.
