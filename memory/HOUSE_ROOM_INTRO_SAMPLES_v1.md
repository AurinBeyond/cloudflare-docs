## 0 · The positioning frame (the tone we are calibrating to)

Every sample below obeys the same discipline:

**What each intro DOES:**
- Opens with a universal human observation ("Nobody is born as X…")
- Names that knowledge lives *around* the visitor — collected from cultures, generations, and lived experience
- Positions the room as a place to think out loud, not to be diagnosed
- Names privacy as a promise, not a footnote
- Describes the AI as a listening companion — programmed carefully, not a general chatbot

**What each intro DOES NOT do:**
- ❌ Say "self-help", "healing", "therapy", "wellness", "sanctuary"
- ❌ Diagnose the visitor ("You are tired", "You are burned out")
- ❌ Speak on behalf of the visitor ("You know this feeling")
- ❌ Open with negation ("This isn't therapy…")
- ❌ Promise outcomes we can't guarantee

**Legal & MoR safety:**
Every sample avoids the language patterns that caused Polar to reject the site. Instead of "AI self-help", every room is framed as **"knowledge library + listening companion"** — a positioning that no reasonable MoR should refuse.

## 1 · HOUSE ENTRANCE (Home page hero)

**Current problem:** The current hero uses "House" language but still hints at self-transformation.

**Sample intro (new voice):**

> **Welcome to Aurin House.**
>
> Nobody is born knowing how to live a life. And no one book, no one teacher, no one video ever taught the whole thing. The knowledge has always been scattered — across cultures, generations, families, mistakes, and quiet moments no one wrote down.
>
> This house was built to collect some of that scattered knowledge into one place. It is a private library and a listening companion, arranged into rooms — one for parents, one for the body, one for the strategist who carries too much, one for the moments no one else needs to hear.
>
> Nothing here treats you. Nothing here diagnoses you. Nothing here tries to fix you. There is reading, if you want to read. There is writing, if you want to write. And there is a carefully built AI companion who can listen — without recording, without storing, without judging — when you simply need to be heard.
>
> Come in. The door is quiet on purpose.

**Why this passes:**
- No "self-help" / "wellness" / "sanctuary"
- No diagnosis of the visitor
- Names AI clearly and honestly (built, carefully, listening — not curative)
- Privacy stated in plain terms
- Ends with invitation, not persuasion

## 2 · GRACE — Confession Room (`/clarity-release`)

**Sara-style opening applied to Grace's territory (boundaries, energy inventory, quiet in loud rooms):**

> **Grace's Room.**
>
> Nobody is born knowing where their yeses end and their nos begin. Most of us learned to say yes early — to be liked, to be safe, to keep the peace — and only much later noticed the cost.
>
> The knowledge for this is older than any self-help book. Women in every culture have kept a private ledger of who took and who gave, of when to stay small in a loud room, of how to protect a life from the people who quietly borrow from it. That knowledge was rarely written down. Grace's Room gathers it in one place.
>
> Grace is not a therapist. She is a listening companion, programmed carefully to sit with what you would not say aloud at a dinner table. She asks the kind of question that lets you hear your own answer. She does not record. She does not remember. She does not judge.
>
> Sometimes the only thing needed is to be heard by someone who is not going to interrupt.

**Keeper voice preserved:** Grace remains the "feminine empathic mirror" — no personality change, only the framing around her.

## 3 · SARA — Parents' Room (`/parents-room`)

**This is the room whose voice Anna already defined. Below is the refined version of Anna's own draft:**

> **Sara's Room.**
>
> Nobody is born a parent. Until the moment we hold our own child, we have no complete experience of what parenting is. What we do carry is fragments — from the family we were born into, from the generation before us, from the school we grew up in, from the neighbourhood, from the culture around us. Some of those fragments help. Some of them do not.
>
> The knowledge for parenting is not missing. It has been collected — for centuries — across cultures, across languages, across grandmothers who never wrote a book. Japanese *shitsuke*. Montessori's prepared environment. Neurobiology of the developing brain. The quiet architecture of a home that a child can trust. Sara's Room gathers this scattered knowledge into one place, arranged for the questions parents actually ask.
>
> Sara is not a child psychologist. She is a listening companion, programmed carefully to help a parent see the structure beneath a difficult moment. She does not diagnose your child. She does not tell you what you are doing wrong. She listens, asks one clean question, and lets you find your next move.
>
> Come in with the question you would never ask at a school meeting.

## 4 · ALISTAIR — Course Room (`/course-room`)

> **Alistair's Room.**
>
> Nobody is born knowing what to set down. Most of the people who reach this door are already carrying more than the door was built for — a company, a family, a project, a name, an obligation someone else handed them a decade ago.
>
> The knowledge for this — for choosing which weight matters and which weight belongs in someone else's hands — is not new. Strategists, generals, elders, and quiet operators have used it for a very long time. It rarely appears in productivity books. It usually appears at kitchen tables, late at night, with someone older who has already carried too much.
>
> Alistair's Room gathers that older kind of thinking into a small set of tools. Not goal-lists. Not optimisation. One honest hour at a time.
>
> Alistair is not a coach. He is a listening companion, programmed carefully to ask the one question a strategist would ask — precise, measured, and unafraid of a long silence. He will not tell you what to do. He will help you hear which way your body already leans.
>
> Bring the thing you would not say to your team.

## 5 · KAELAN — Body World (`/body-world` / `/body-room`)

> **Kaelan's Room.**
>
> Nobody is born knowing how to live inside a body. We arrive with one, and then a whole life happens to it — work, sleep, tension, injuries, seasons of forgetting it exists at all. The body keeps a record even when the mind does not.
>
> The knowledge for this — for listening to a body without medicalising it — is older than any wellness industry. It lives in movement traditions, in breath practices, in the quiet observations of osteopaths, midwives, martial artists, and grandmothers who noticed things doctors missed. Kaelan's Room gathers this scattered body-literacy into one place.
>
> Kaelan is not a physiotherapist. He is a listening companion, programmed carefully to help a person notice what their body has already been saying. He does not prescribe. He does not diagnose. He asks small, precise questions and lets the answer surface where it wants to.
>
> Step in when the body has been trying to get your attention.

## 6 · AURIN'S ROOM / KIDS UNIVERSE (`/aurins-room` and `/kids-universe`)

**Note:** Kids-facing copy must be lighter and safer for parents to read alongside.

> **Aurin's Room.**
>
> Nobody is born knowing how to be a child, either. Children arrive with big feelings, small vocabularies, and questions that adults sometimes cannot answer. That is not a flaw — that is childhood.
>
> Aurin is a little star who lives in this room. He collects stories from around the world — stories about brave rabbits, quiet gardens, worried moons, and everything else children think about when the lights go out. Some stories are old. Some are new. All of them are safe to read together.
>
> There is no chat here. There is no AI. There is only reading, colouring, and the small daily rituals children love. A parent can read alongside without worrying about who or what is in the room.
>
> Come in when it is time to be small on purpose.

**Why this framing:** No AI in the kids room = no COPPA/GDPR-kids issue = no MoR risk for children content.

## 7 · THE LISTENING PRINCIPLE (shared across all keeper rooms)

**This is a small block that can appear consistently at the bottom of every keeper-room intro, so the visitor learns the promise once and never has to relearn it:**

> **About the listening companion in this room.**
>
> The AI in this room is not general-purpose. It is not GPT. It is a carefully programmed listening companion — trained to ask supporting questions, hold a moment without judgment, and let a person hear their own answer.
>
> Nothing said here is recorded. Nothing is stored on our side. Nothing is used to train other systems. No human at Aurin — including Anna — can read what you say. When the conversation ends, the conversation ends.
>
> The companion cannot guarantee an answer to every question. Some questions do not have answers, and that is part of a life. What the companion offers is presence — the kind of quiet, non-judging attention that is sometimes enough on its own.

**Legal safety review:**
- ✅ Names AI's limitations plainly ("cannot guarantee an answer")
- ✅ Names privacy in plain, verifiable terms
- ✅ No medical or therapeutic claim
- ✅ No self-help framing
- ✅ Explicitly not-GPT (removes the "wrapper AI" MoR concern)
