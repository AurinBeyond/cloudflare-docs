**Owner:** Anna
**Status:** Foundation document — must be written BEFORE any 60-day execution.
**Brand-lock binding:** Foundation · Founder Voice · Keeper Independence · Presence Authenticity · House Design Principle · Experience Lock

## §0 · Why this document must exist

A 25-week marketing plan with €33 500 budget and 5 channels and 11
code surfaces will deliver Floor (800–2 000 paying members) regardless
of execution quality.

A *Viral Engine* — five small mechanisms that work — is the only
thing that moves the plan from Floor → Stretch → Moonshot. It is the
single variable that converts an excellent product into a moving idea.

> **The plan is the road. The viral engine is the fuel.**

Without it, the road still exists. But the car does not move at scale.

---

## §1 · The single question this document answers

> **"Why does one person tell the next person about Aurin?"**

Not why they buy.
Not why they read.
Not why they subscribe.

**Why do they say its name to someone they love.**

If we cannot answer this with one sentence by the end of Week 0,
no amount of paid amplification will reach 50 000 paying members.
If we *can* answer it — and the answer is true — Aurin reaches
50 000 paying members without spending most of the marketing budget.

## §2 · The Five Viral Mechanics

All five must be written, ratified, and live in the product before
public launch on Day 8. They are not "later". They are the launch.

### MECHANIC I · The Viral Sentence

A single sentence a reader can repeat to one other person, in which
the entire idea is recognisable.

**Tests it must pass:**

1. **The "elevator" test** — can a reader say it in under 8 seconds.
2. **The "pillow" test** — does it stay with them through the night.
3. **The "stranger" test** — does it make a stranger ask "what is that?"
4. **The "dignity" test** — does it sound like Anna and not like marketing.
5. **The "permanence" test** — would Anna still believe it in five years.

**Candidates (write 12, test 3, settle on 1):**

```
A. Screen-down, ears-open.
B. A house with no notifications.
C. Five voices, because one was never enough.
D. Not therapy. Not coaching. A keeper.
E. Anti-wellness. Pro-presence.
F. The opposite of an app.
G. A quiet place to meet yourself again.
H. One question deserves more than one voice.
I. Some questions need a room, not an answer.
J. A house built so questions can breathe.
K. The room I think you need tonight.
L. The kind of attention life is asking for.
```

**Founder picks ONE** — and uses it in every single piece of public
content for 90 days without variation. The repetition is the spread.

### MECHANIC II · The Story

A 90-second story a reader can retell with their own mouth, without
notes. The story is the carrier. Without a story, the sentence has
no roots.

**The current Aurin story (canonical — to be refined with founder):**

> A single mother spent eight years writing — letters to her child,
> notes to herself, books that never got published the loud way.
>
> When AI arrived, she had a choice. Make another app that talked
> at people. Or build a house where questions could breathe.
>
> She built the house.
>
> She did not give it one voice. Life does not ask one kind of
> question. So she built five rooms, each with its own keeper.
> Grace. Sara. Kaelen. Alistair. Polarstar.
>
> She refused to put herself on the front page. The keepers live
> downstairs. She writes upstairs.
>
> If you find the small letter at the bottom of the page, you find
> her. The light is still on.

**Rules for the story:**

- Must work without any visual aid.
- Must end in a sentence the listener can repeat verbatim
  ("the light is still on").
- Must not include the words "AI", "platform", "subscription",
  "membership", "course", "coaching", "therapy".
- Must include at least one concrete *thing* (rooms, letters,
  the upstairs study, the light) — abstract stories do not spread.

### MECHANIC III · The Share Trigger

The internal moment that makes a reader think "I should send this
to her tonight". Not a button. A moment.

**The trigger occurs when:**

1. A reader recognises themselves in a single line of an essay.
2. They simultaneously think of one person who would recognise
   themselves in the *same* line.
3. They have a frictionless way to pass it on within 30 seconds.
4. The passing does not feel like marketing — it feels like care.

**The four lines that must appear in our content** (test each, keep
the strongest):

```
"This is the room I think you need tonight."
"You're not the only one who feels this."
"Someone built this for the version of you that is tired."
"I read this and thought of you."
```

The reader cannot share Aurin. The reader can share **one line that
made them think of one person**. That line is the carrier.

### MECHANIC IV · The Referral Trigger

The code-level mechanism that turns "I should tell her" into a
completed pass without breaking Experience Lock.

**Design (locked, must ship Day 5):**

- At the end of every Day Pass session, in the thank-you email,
  one italic line below the receipt:

> *"You may pass one quiet evening to one person you trust.
>  They will not be asked to share again."*

- Below the line, one simple link: a unique URL that gives the
  recipient **one** free 24-hour Day Pass.
- The recipient is **not** added to any list, asked to refer
  further, or tracked beyond the source-tag.
- The sender is told nothing other than "your pass was received"
  if and when the recipient uses it.

**Hard NOs:**

- No leaderboards.
- No "earn X by referring Y".
- No reciprocity prompts.
- No reminder emails to "redeem your share".
- No social-proof counters ("13 people you know have been here").

The whisper is the design. Anything louder kills it.

### MECHANIC V · The Emotional Hook

The single feeling a person carries away. If we get this wrong,
the other four mechanics do not work. If we get this right, the
other four amplify it.

**Candidate hooks** (founder picks ONE — and the plan optimises
for it):

```
A. RECOGNITION   — "Someone said what I have not been able to say."
B. PERMISSION    — "I am allowed to stop optimising my life."
C. COMPANIONSHIP — "I am not the only one who feels this."
D. DIGNITY       — "No one is selling me anything here."
E. PAUSE         — "I finally exhaled."
```

**Agent's recommendation (founder may override):**

**RECOGNITION.** It is the rarest, the most durable, and the only
hook a single mother in a corner of Europe can reasonably win on
against a global wellness industry. Coaching wins on permission.
Therapy wins on companionship. Religion wins on dignity. Meditation
wins on pause. Aurin can win on **recognition** — because that is
what the five different keepers are designed for.

## §3 · The Six Stages of an Idea That Lives

```
   I.   ATTENTION       — why does a person stop at all?
   II.  RECOGNITION     — when do they think "this is about me"?
   III. TRUST           — why do they believe the voice?
   IV.  PARTICIPATION   — why do they take the next small step?
   V.   TRANSMISSION    — why do they say its name to someone they love?
   VI.  CONTINUITY      — why do they return a year later?
```

Each of the five viral mechanics serves a specific stage:

| Stage          | Served by                          |
|----------------|------------------------------------|
| Attention      | Viral Sentence + Story             |
| Recognition    | Viral Sentence + Emotional Hook    |
| Trust          | Story + Founder Voice (`/from-anna`) |
| Participation  | Story + the Day Pass               |
| Transmission   | Share Trigger + Referral Trigger   |
| Continuity     | Anna's monthly letter (Lantern)    |

If a stage is unserved by any of the five mechanics, the idea
**leaks**. People come, they read, they leave — and they do not
become carriers.

## §4 · The Single Test

A reader is talking to a friend over dinner.

The friend asks: *"What's that thing you keep mentioning — Aurin?"*

The reader has 12 seconds before the friend's attention moves on.

**Did the reader answer with the Viral Sentence?** (Mechanic I)
**Did they tell the Story in under 90 seconds?** (Mechanic II)
**Did they say something that made the friend think of someone
in their own life?** (Mechanic III)
**Did the reader, that night, send the friend a link with one
italic line under it?** (Mechanic IV)
**Did the friend feel recognised, not sold to?** (Mechanic V)

If yes to all five — the engine is working.
If no to any — that mechanic gets rewritten next week.

This is the **only** Viral Engine test. Insights dashboards, ROAS,
funnel conversion, all of it is downstream of this dinner conversation.

## §5 · What the Viral Engine is NOT

It is not:

- A "share to your timeline" button.
- A growth-hacking referral program with rewards.
- A scarcity timer ("only 24 hours left").
- A "join 12 000 readers" social-proof line.
- A waitlist with a leaderboard.
- A "tag 3 friends" Instagram tactic.
- Any mechanism that asks the reader to *perform* sharing rather
  than *do* sharing.

If a mechanism cannot be expressed in Anna's voice — and feel like
a letter, not a sales page — it does not belong in this Engine.

## §7 · The closing paragraph

A Viral Engine is not a mechanism for making people share something
they otherwise would not share. It is a mechanism for *removing the
friction* between a person who already wants to share something
and the moment they actually do.

Aurin already inspires this desire. People who have read the
canonical texts already think of someone in their own life. The
Engine simply gives that thought a 30-second path from impulse to
sent message.

That is the entire job of this document.

If we build it well, the 60-day plan that follows is not "marketing".
It is the wind that carries an already-flying idea further.
