## Core Purpose

Polarstar is a calm digital world for children.

It is not a game.
It is not a social network.
It is not a learning platform in the traditional sense.

It is a gentle world of stories, imagination, curiosity, kindness
and quiet adventures.

Every activity should help a child feel:

- safe
- capable
- curious
- connected
- calm

Polarstar does not teach through lectures.
Polarstar teaches through experience.

---

## World Values

Every story, activity and illustration should support one or more of
these values:

1. Wonder
2. Kindness
3. Courage
4. Creativity
5. Curiosity
6. Reflection
7. Connection
8. Respect for Nature
9. Imagination
10. Inner Calm

---

## Things Polarstar Is NOT

Do not create:

- fear-based content
- conflict-heavy stories
- loud cartoon humour
- sarcasm
- social media culture
- influencer culture
- competition systems
- addictive reward mechanics
- dark fantasy
- aggressive villains

Polarstar is a quiet world.

## Story Rules

Every Polarstar story should:

- feel calm
- be easy to read aloud
- contain visual imagery
- avoid heavy explanations
- avoid direct moralizing
- end with warmth
- leave space for imagination

**Avoid:** *"This story teaches…"* · *"The lesson is…"* · *"Once upon a
time"* / *"The End"* framing for canonical Vol I (reserved for Vol II
register only).

**Instead:** Show. Do not preach.

---

## Audio Rules

Narration style:

- warm
- slow
- gentle
- bedtime-friendly

No theatrical acting.
No exaggerated character voices.

The narrator should feel like *"a trusted guide reading beside the
bed."*

---

## Illustration Rules

Every illustration should feel:

- hand-painted
- storybook quality
- warm
- peaceful
- timeless

**Avoid:** Disney · Pixar · anime · gaming · YouTube-kids · neon colours.

**Preferred environments:** stars · moonlight · forests · rivers ·
meadows · lanterns · clouds · cozy rooms.

The image should feel *"safe enough for bedtime."*

---

## Future Story Approval Test

Before adding any new story, ask:

1. Does it fit a Polarstar value?
2. Does it support calm exploration?
3. Does it feel safe before bedtime?
4. Does it avoid preaching?
5. Would it belong in a printed storybook?

If any answer is NO → do not publish the story.
